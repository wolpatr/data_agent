Glossary — the agent's vocabulary

These definitions are canonical: every lesson in this course uses them exactly like this. Term claims cite a source where one exists.

pair (e.g. BTC/USDT)
The two assets being exchanged: base (BTC) against quote (USDT). Buying the pair means spending quote to hold base. — CCXT manual
spot
Trading the asset itself, not contracts on it; no leverage, no expiry. Your agent's sandbox for now.
OHLCV (candle)
Open, High, Low, Close, Volume for one time interval (a timeframe, e.g. 1h). The standard unit of market history you will fetch with fetchOHLCV.
ticker
The cheapest possible market snapshot: last price, bid/ask. What you Observe between candle closes.
order book
The exchange's pending limit orders (ask side / bid side). The gap between best bid and best ask is the spread.
market order
"Trade now at whatever the book offers." Guaranteed fill (if liquidity exists), not a guaranteed price. Slippage costs more when the order is big.
limit order
"Trade only at my price or better." Price-controlled but may never fill. Classic autonomy trade-off: market orders finish, limit orders negotiate.
maker / taker fees (bps)
Per-trade fee in basis points (1 bps = 0.01%). Taker = you cross the spread (market orders, aggressive limits). Maker = you provide liquidity. Fees are why Lesson 0002 exists: research shows naive sign-switching strategies are profitable until fees arrive.[1]
EMA (exponential moving average)
A weighted average of past prices that favours recent prices. Widely used signal basis; crossovers of a fast and a slow EMA are the textbook trend signal.[2]
position
What you currently hold. Flat (no exposure) or long (holding the base asset). Exposure changes only when you Execute.
signal
The Decide module's output: BUY / SELL / HOLD plus its reason. The reason is not decoration — it is your audit trail.
backtest
Replay the agent's decisions on historical data as if it had run then. The cheapest feedback loop — and the easiest one to cheat (that's Lesson 0002's topic).
dry-run / paper trading
Run the real agent, real data, real API — but fake money and simulated fills. The last rung before live.[3]
live trading
Real money. Reached only after backtest → paper both show the system behaving as expected.
drawdown
Drop from the highest equity point reached so far. Risk-management language is written in drawdown, not in returns alone.
Sharpe ratio
Return earned per unit of risk (excess return ÷ volatility). The trading-metric papers use it to rank strategies[4] — because +65%/yr with Sharpe < 1 is a different beast than with Sharpe > 1.
walk-forward
Train/tune on a past window, test on the next unseen window, roll everything forward, repeat 20–30×. Research's answer to "why did my backtest lie?"[1]
turnover
How much notional value trades per unit time. High turnover × fee bps = a silent strategy killer[1].
